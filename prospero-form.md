In place of a human research team, the review uses a team of large language model roles, set out in the subsection below and in the protocol. The model roles are tools under the author direction. They are not team members in the PROSPERO sense, hold no authorship, and carry no accountability, which rests with the human author alone.

### LLM team in place of a human research team
A Cochrane author team has a search specialist, two screeners who work apart, two extractors, a risk of bias pair, a statistician, a GRADE assessor and a methods editor. This review maps each job to a named language model role: information specialist; screener A and screener B; adjudicator; extractor A and extractor B; risk of bias assessor pair; statistician; GRADE assessor; methods editor.
Independence rules: screener A and screener B work in separate passes with separate prompts, and neither sees the other calls until both passes are complete. The same rule holds for the extractor pair and the risk of bias pair. A merge step done by code counts agreements and disagreements and makes no judgement. The adjudicator settles screener disagreements with a logged reason. Items it cannot settle go to the human author.
Human oversight: the author set the question and eligibility rules, settles flagged items and all risk of bias and GRADE disagreements, checks every pooled number against its source, and signs off the final numbers.
Disclosure, RAISE 2025: tool, version, dates, prompts and verification steps are logged in the review team log and reported in the finished review, in line with the 2025 RAISE statement on responsible AI use in evidence synthesis.
Mechanics reported as results: inter-screener agreement rate and Cohen kappa at title and abstract, count and type of disagreements, adjudication outcomes, and extraction discrepancy rate. These counts are reported in the review methods and results.

## 15. Review question
To assess the effects of ambient AI scribes, compared with usual documentation, dictation or manual typing, on documentation time, after-hours work, clinician burnout, note quality and note errors in outpatient and inpatient care.

## 18. Condition or domain being studied
Clinician documentation burden in health care, and the accuracy of AI generated clinical notes. Population health and patient outcomes are in scope only where a study ties them to encounter documentation with an ambient scribe, for example patient experience of the encounter.

## 19. Participants and population
Include: clinicians who document patient encounters in any specialty and any country, including physicians and advanced practice clinicians. Mixed clinician groups included. Exclude: studies whose participants are only students, human scribes, patients or administrators.

## 20. Intervention, exposure
Ambient AI scribe: software that listens to a clinical encounter, with consent, and drafts a clinical note for clinician review and signature, with no dictation step. Named products include Microsoft DAX Copilot and Dragon Ambient eXperience, Nabla, Abridge and Ambience Healthcare products. Equivalent ambient products are eligible on the same test.

## 21. Comparator and control
Usual documentation, manual typing, dictation, speech recognition, human scribe, no scribe, or a pre-implementation period in before and after studies.

## 22. Types of study to be included
Randomised trials including cluster and stepped wedge designs. Non-randomised studies with a concurrent comparator. Uncontrolled before and after studies and interrupted time series, synthesised apart from controlled studies. Note audit studies grading a defined sample of scribe notes against the encounter record, audio or a human reference note, with a denominator in notes.
Exclude: qualitative studies with no listed quantitative outcome, editorials, commentaries, opinion pieces, protocols with no results, systematic and scoping reviews (used for reference checking only), case reports, vendor white papers with no independently reported data.

## 23. Context
Any health care setting where clinicians document encounters: outpatient, inpatient, emergency and virtual care, primary and secondary care, any country. Classroom and lab studies with simulated patients are excluded from the main synthesis, except standardised note audit studies that use real encounter recordings and report error counts per note, which are included for the error outcomes and flagged in extraction.

## 24. Main outcomes
Documentation time, minutes per note, per encounter or per day, from electronic health record audit logs or timed observation. After-hours documentation time per day or week. Burnout and work exhaustion on a validated scale. Note errors per note audited, reported apart as omissions, hallucinations or fabrications, and wrong inclusions.
Timing: time points grouped as up to 90 days and over 90 days. Latest time point in each group used in the main analysis.

## 25. Additional outcomes
Note quality on a named instrument such as PDQI-9 or QNOTE, with grader and blinding extracted. Professional fulfillment on a validated scale. Time to note closure. Patient experience of the encounter on a named measure. Note length in words or characters, reported as a cost signal.

## 26. Data extraction, selection and coding
Two stage screening, title and abstract then full text, against the criteria in fields 19 to 23. Piloted extraction form covers study identity, country, setting, specialty, design, clinician and encounter counts, product and version, consent model, comparator, follow-up, each outcome with measure and time point, note audit method and grader, funding, vendor role and stated conflicts.
Automation, LLM team: screening is done by two independent language model screener passes, A and B, with separate prompts and no sight of each other calls until both are done. An adjudicator pass settles disagreements with a logged reason, and items it cannot settle go to the human author. Extraction is done by two independent extractor passes and compared field by field, with each mismatch logged and settled against the source. The author checks every number that enters a pool. Tool, version, dates and prompts are logged in the team log. Inter-screener agreement, Cohen kappa, disagreement types, adjudication outcomes and extraction discrepancy rate are reported in the review. Full detail sits in the protocol, in the subsection titled LLM team in place of a human research team.

## 27. Risk of bias assessment
RoB 2 for randomised trials, five domains, effect of assignment. ROBINS-I for non-randomised studies, seven domains, with confounding set in advance: baseline documentation load, specialty mix, concurrent record system changes, and self-selection of keen users into scribe groups. Judgements are made per outcome where data allow, by two independent assessor passes, with disagreements settled by the human author and counted in the team log. Plans follow the 2025 RAISE statement for AI help in the review process, with human oversight of every judgement.

## 28. Strategy for data synthesis
Random effects meta-analysis, inverse variance, in R. Mean difference where measures match, standardised mean difference where scales differ, with direction set so lower means less burnout before pooling. Before and after studies pooled apart from controlled studies. Error outcomes pooled as proportions of notes with at least one error of each type, types kept apart, and error counts per note pooled as rates apart from proportions. Tau squared, I squared and, at ten or more studies, a prediction interval reported for each pool. Where pooling would mix unlike measures, structured narrative synthesis is used instead.
Subgroups set in advance: product, specialty, study design, vendor funding or authorship, follow-up under 90 days against 90 days or more, clinician type. Sensitivity analyses set in advance: controlled studies only, low or moderate risk of bias only, leave one product out, preprints removed, single item burnout measures removed. Funnel plot and Egger test only at ten or more studies in a pool.
