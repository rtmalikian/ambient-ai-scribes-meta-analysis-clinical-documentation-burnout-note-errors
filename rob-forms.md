# Risk of bias forms

Two independent assessor passes per study, working apart. One form per study per outcome where the data allow. Agreements stand. A disagreement, with domain and outcome named, goes to the human author, whose settlement and reason are recorded on the form. Counts of disagreements go to llm-team-log.md.

## Form 1: RoB 2, for randomised trials

Study ID. Outcome assessed. Time point. Effect of interest: assignment to the ambient scribe.

Domain 1, bias from the randomisation process.
- Signalling answers, the allocation sequence, concealment, and baseline differences that suggest a problem.
- Judgement: low, some concerns, high.
Domain 2, bias due to deviations from intended interventions.
- Signalling answers, awareness of assignment (expected in scribe trials, since a clinician knows a tool is listening), deviations that arose from the trial context, and analysis type.
- Judgement: low, some concerns, high.
Domain 3, bias due to missing outcome data.
- Signalling answers, share of clinicians or encounters with outcome data, and whether missingness could rest on the true value. Audit log outcomes and survey outcomes are judged apart, since survey loss in this field is heavy and self-selected.
- Judgement: low, some concerns, high.
Domain 4, bias in measurement of the outcome.
- Signalling answers, who measured, whether the measurer knew the assignment, and whether the measure itself can shift with knowledge. Audit log minutes are hard to shift. Self reported burnout from volunteers is not.
- Judgement: low, some concerns, high.
Domain 5, bias in selection of the reported result.
- Signalling answers, a registration or analysis plan to check against, and signs of picking among measures, time points or subgroups.
- Judgement: low, some concerns, high.
Overall judgement for this outcome, set by the RoB 2 rules from the five domains, with the deciding domains named.
Support for each judgement: quote or page reference, one per domain. A judgement with no support text does not count.

## Form 2: ROBINS-I, for non-randomised studies

Study ID. Outcome assessed. Time point. Comparison: scribe against the comparator named on the extraction form.

Pre-set confounding for this review, from the protocol: baseline documentation load, specialty mix, concurrent record system changes, self-selection of keen users into scribe groups. Pre-set co-interventions: documentation training, template changes, staffing changes in the same period.

Domain 1, bias due to confounding.
- Which of the pre-set confounders the study handled, which it left open, and the likely direction of what is left.
- Judgement: low, moderate, serious, critical, no information.
Domain 2, bias in selection of participants.
- How scribe users were picked or picked themselves, and whether selection rested on things tied to the outcome.
- Judgement as above.
Domain 3, bias in classification of interventions.
- Whether scribe use was defined and measured cleanly, including mixed use and crossover between products.
- Judgement as above.
Domain 4, bias due to deviations from intended interventions.
- Co-interventions from the pre-set list, and switches away from the scribe in the follow-up window.
- Judgement as above.
Domain 5, bias due to missing data.
- Share missing per outcome, reasons given, and handling.
- Judgement as above.
Domain 6, bias in measurement of outcomes.
- Measure quality, grader knowledge for note quality and error audits, and self report issues for burnout.
- Judgement as above.
Domain 7, bias in selection of the reported result.
- Registration or plan to check against, and picking among measures or time points.
- Judgement as above.
Overall judgement for this outcome, set by the ROBINS-I rules, worst domain carries the study, with the deciding domains named.
Support for each judgement: quote or page reference, one per domain.

## Note audit studies, addition to either form
Where the study is a note audit, add three lines to Domain 6 (ROBINS-I) or Domain 4 (RoB 2): how the audited notes were sampled, what reference the notes were graded against, and whether the grader knew the tool. An audit graded against the tool own transcript, with no encounter reference, is a measurement problem and is judged as such.
